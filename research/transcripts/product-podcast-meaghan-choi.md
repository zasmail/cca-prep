## Recap: "How Claude Code Hit $2.5B in Year One" (Product School Substack)
Six insights attributed to Choi, drawn from her ProductCon New York stage talk (same core
material as the podcast):

1. **Role flexibility over rigid titles.** Anthropic organizes work in small pods of three to
   five people with varying compositions. Choi: "titles signal a specialty vs the scope of work"
   — team members contribute across functions without waiting for permission to work outside
   traditional boundaries.
2. **Quality decisions move to working code.** Rather than evaluating products at the PRD or
   design-mockup stage, Anthropic shifted quality gates to "live working code" experienced in
   actual workflows, which reveals richer signals than documents alone.
3. **Internal adoption as a measurable launch gate.** Claude Code stayed unreleased externally
   until the team observed "a specific number of internal daily active users" genuinely adopting
   it — turning customer validation into a measurable threshold rather than a philosophy.
4. **Bottom-up enterprise growth.** Individual developers adopting Claude Code in personal
   projects became internal advocates, expanding usage inside organizations without a top-down
   sales motion.
5. **Traditional metrics still matter.** Choi advocates returning to "adoption, retention,
   revenue" as the true ROI indicators, not token-usage counts.
6. **ICs as agent managers.** Team members now function as "mini managers" directing multiple AI
   agents simultaneously, creating compounding productivity leverage.

## Recap: tldrecap.tech ("Why Your Whole Team Should Be Managing Agents")
Additional detail consistent with the above, from the same talk/podcast press cycle:
- Claude Code originated in 2024 as an internal experiment: engineers explored letting Claude
  write code autonomously to eliminate manual copy-paste between interfaces.
- Anthropic has notable female leadership across senior roles (CPO, Head of Product, President
  cited in the recap).
- Choi, quoted directly: **"I am pushing code to production, and my engineers are designing."**
- Optimal team size: three to five people mixing engineers, designers, and PMs, with
  democratized shipping capability.
- "Customer Zero" approach: mandates internal-adoption metrics before external release.
- Quality assessment shifted from design documents to "live, working code" experienced in real
  workflows.
- Enterprise security: Claude Code built with inherent safety mechanisms and "hackability"
  controls, aimed at regulated industries without sacrificing accessibility.
- Market performance figure cited: $2.5B revenue in year one, ~51% share of the coding-tool
  market.
