# Animals vs Ghosts — Andrej Karpathy (summary)

Published October 1, 2025.

## Framing

The essay reacts to a Dwarkesh Patel interview with Richard Sutton about
competing philosophies for building intelligent systems.

## Key arguments, in order

1. **Sutton's critique of LLMs.** Sutton questions whether LLMs really
   follow "the bitter lesson" (capability scales with compute), since they
   depend on a finite, human-generated dataset and human-curated
   fine-tuning at multiple stages.

2. **Sutton's alternative — "child machines."** Systems that learn through
   world interaction via reinforcement learning, modeled on animal
   cognition, driven by intrinsic motivation (curiosity, fun) rather than
   supervised fine-tuning (which doesn't occur naturally in animals).

3. **Karpathy's pushback.** Purely unsupervised/RL learning may not be
   sufficient on its own: animal brains carry an evolved genetic
   initialization — effectively billions of hard-coded parameters from
   evolution. Karpathy frames today's pretraining step as a practical
   stand-in for that biological head start.

4. **Core thesis — "animals vs. ghosts."** Today's LLMs are "ghosts":
   statistical distillations of human-written internet text, shaped by
   human influence, and fundamentally different from biological "animals."
   He floats the possibility that ghosts might eventually acquire
   animal-like qualities, or might stay permanently distinct — analogous to
   how planes diverged from birds rather than converging with them.

5. **Practical implications.** The frontier research community may
   over-index on benchmarks; ideas drawn from animal cognition — multi-agent
   interaction, culture, "empowerment" — are underexplored and worth more
   attention.

## Short attributed quotes

- "Pretraining is our crappy evolution." — Karpathy
